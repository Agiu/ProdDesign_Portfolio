

## Overview | ==Deciding what to play together takes longer than playing it.==

Cloud gaming solved the hardware problem. You no longer need the console, the install, or the patch — the library is already there, a click away, on whatever screen is closest. What it did not solve is the part that actually stalls a game night: ==four people in a group chat, a library of thousands of titles, and nobody willing to be the one who picks.==

![Xbox Arcade — a cloud gaming nook built around group discovery](https://media.kaelub.com/Xbox/Xbox_casestudy_hero.jpg)

This is our eight-month capstone with **Xbox**, exploring what a cloud gaming *nook* could be: a shared surface where a friend group discovers, decides, and drops into a game together, built on the seam between where players already hang out (Discord) and where the games already live (Xbox Cloud Gaming).

## The Problem

Discovery surfaces are built for one person. Storefronts, recommendation rails, and wishlists all assume a single player with a single taste profile, scrolling alone. ==A friend group has to run that single-player interface in serial —== one person shares their screen, everyone else reacts, and the decision gets made by whoever gets tired of deliberating first.

The cost is not just time. It is the quiet attrition of a group that logged on to play something and spent the evening negotiating instead.

## Designing for the Group, Not the Player

Two of the pieces I led prototyping and strategy on sit directly on that problem:

```insights
Immediate Group Decision-Making | A tool that collapses the negotiation into a single shared moment, so a group converges on a title in seconds rather than trading suggestions across a chat thread. | Users
Adaptive Information Cards | Game cards that change what they surface depending on who is in the group and what category they are browsing — the details that matter to this set of players, rather than a fixed spec sheet. | SplitSquareHorizontal
```

The nook framing matters here. Rather than treating discovery as a page you visit, we treated it as a place the group occupies together — which changes what the interface owes them. It has to hold four people's attention at once, resolve quickly, and get out of the way.

## Bringing It to Pitch

Alongside the product work, I owned the art and design direction for the hi-fi mockups and built the motion design into the prototype, so the flows read as a real product rather than a sequence of screens.

We co-directed and produced a product pitch positioned for the Xbox and Discord partnership, ==with funding from Netflix.==
